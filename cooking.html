Choose your meals first. Cook the right amount, measure the finished batch and freeze the portions you will eat later.

Start with the actual menu

  1. Choose complete days or meal swaps in Meals.
  2. Open Shop, check the selected dates and subtract what is already in your kitchen.
  3. Gather a scale, food thermometer, shallow containers and freezer labels. Make room in the fridge and freezer before cooking.
  4. Count portions from this menu. There is no fixed number of boxes: your recipes and dates determine the batch.

Bulk package cost and weekly consumption cost are different. Check shelf prices and labels; old price snapshots are not current quotes.

The Sunday order

  1. Read the recipe quantities. Weigh chicken before cooking and rice, lentils, oats or pasta dry when specified. Include sauce ingredients in recipe totals.
  2. Start the longest jobs. Cook lentils and grains according to their package instructions. Keep raw poultry and its utensils separate from ready-to-eat food.
  3. Cook the protein. Season chicken or turkey for the chosen recipe. Check the thickest part with a thermometer: poultry pieces and ground poultry need 74°C. Health Canada cooking temperatures.
  4. Prepare the extras. Cook vegetables, mix a cold sauce in a clean bowl and portion breakfast ingredients. Leave crisp toppings separate when useful.
  5. Measure finished yields. Weigh each cooked ingredient without the pot. Allocate portions using the formula below.
  6. Label and chill promptly. Write the recipe, cooking date and intended meal date. Follow the rice rule for every box containing rice.

Raw portions → cooked servings

Cooked serving = cooked batch weight × (raw or dry portion ÷ raw or dry batch weight).

Example: 500 g dry rice becomes 1,400 g cooked. A recipe calling for 100 g dry-equivalent rice gets 1,400 × 100 ÷ 500 = 280 g cooked. If the next batch absorbs more water, weigh it again rather than repeating 280 g.

Apply the same method to chicken and lentils separately. It allocates recipe portions; it does not prove exact nutrient retention. Weigh oil and peanut butter instead of guessing spoonfuls.

The storage rule to remember

Rice gets the shortest clock. Cool quickly, ideally within one hour. Refrigerate for no more than 24 hours before reheating, or cool and freeze later portions promptly, aiming for within one hour of cooking. Thaw in the fridge, reheat only once and eat immediately after reheating. Food Standards Agency rice guidance.

For other mixed leftovers, use shallow containers, refrigerate promptly and always within two hours, keep the fridge at 4°C or colder, and eat within 2–3 days or freeze. Reheat to 74°C, stirring and checking the centre; discard uneaten reheated food. Keep work lunches cold in a fridge or insulated bag with ice packs. Smell cannot establish safety. Health Canada leftovers guidance.

Make the same staples taste different

The recipe library adds combinations such as lemon-cumin chicken with yogurt sauce and yogurt-based breakfasts. Keep wet sauces and crunchy toppings separate until serving. Add lemon or spices after reheating if the flavour needs a lift; measure calorie-containing additions.

Inspiration includes Chicken Farmers of Canada’s shawarma bowl and Oikos yogurt pancakes. The app’s portions and methods are its own adaptations, not copied nutrition values or a claim of physical taste-testing.

Use the full recipes in Meals for ingredients and amounts. Reusing cooked components is a practical planning approach; Canada’s Food Guide has further leftover-planning ideas.
