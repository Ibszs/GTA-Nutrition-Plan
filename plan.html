The four-day routine

Train Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, or keep the sequence around your schedule. Upper days emphasize lats, side delts and upper chest; lower days retain balanced leg and trunk training. Building these muscles can change your proportions, but no routine guarantees a particular shape.

Before the first big lift, do 2–4 progressively heavier warm-up sets with easy reps. Add a lighter rehearsal for later movements when needed. These are not working sets. Use controlled, comfortable movement and stop for sharp or worsening pain.

Take longer when needed to repeat good reps. A small trial in trained men favoured 3-minute over 1-minute rests for several outcomes; it does not establish one perfect rest time. Upper days may use most of the session. If time runs short, prioritize the first three lifts and lateral raises on upper days, and the first three lifts on lower days. Log what you actually did; do not mark missing sets complete or rush heavy lifts to catch up.

Open the workout logger

Know when to add weight

Reps in reserve (RIR) means how many more clean repetitions you think you could complete. Three RIR leaves room; zero means no further clean rep. Weeks 1–2 use about 3 RIR. From week 3, work mostly at 1–2 RIR. Failure is not required on every set: an eight-week lower-body study found similar quadriceps growth with 1–2 RIR and failure, with more acute fatigue at failure. Refalo et al., 2024.

  1. Pick a load that reaches the bottom of the rep range with the prescribed reserve and good form.
  2. Keep that load and add reps gradually. For 4 × 6–10, 10/10/9/8 means keep building.
  3. When every prescribed set reaches the top cleanly at the planned RIR, use the smallest practical increase next time. The logger uses a conservative 2 RIR threshold after week 2.
  4. If a new load drops you below the range, reduce it or use a smaller increment.

Keep equipment, seat height, range of motion, units and load convention consistent. For dumbbells, log one dumbbell; for barbells, include the bar. Follow each exercise’s Setup note. A different machine or variant starts a new comparison. Adding reps and adding load are both reasonable; neither must happen every workout. Progression trial.

Fatigue, substitutions and short weeks

Choose a comfortable alternative in the logger and keep it stable long enough to compare. If performance falls across several sessions while soreness, sleep or motivation worsen, first review food, sleep and outside workload.

A practical lighter week is about 5–7 days with half the usual sets and 3–4 reps left. The logger uses 4 RIR. Resume normal work when recovered. This is a fatigue-management option, not a required week off or a proven growth boost; a trial of complete cessation found no hypertrophy advantage. Deload trial.

Missed a day? Continue the sequence when you can instead of cramming sessions together. Optional easy walks, such as 20 minutes twice weekly, can fit around lifting if restorative. This is a scheduling idea, not an individualized cardio prescription. Ab work trains the trunk; it does not selectively remove waist fat.

The Sunday review

Weigh under similar morning conditions and compare weekly averages. A practical off-season gain range is 0.25–0.5% of body weight weekly—about 0.4–0.85 lb at 170 lb. Prefer the lower end for this leaner-gain goal; it is guidance, not a measured personal optimum. Off-season nutrition review.

The tracker needs six weigh-ins in each of two seven-day windows, at least 12 intake logs across them, and at least 90% calorie adherence within ±10% of target before offering a review signal. Below 0.25% or above 0.5% weekly gain prompts review. Confirm a persistent trend alongside waist, digestion, recovery, training and real intake before considering a small 100–150 kcal change.

Measure waist weekly at the same place and time; compare relaxed photos monthly if useful. Early scale gain can include water and food mass. Faster gain does not prove faster muscle growth: a small eight-week surplus trial found larger skinfold gains with faster weight gain, without consistent extra muscle or strength benefit. Helms et al., 2023.

At weeks 4, 8, 12 and 16, compare matched exercises, recovery and trends. Continue what works. Projections are informational; they never automatically raise calories to meet 195 lb or trigger a cut. No credible plan can promise 25 lb of lean tissue in 16 weeks.

Why this program, and the limits of the evidence

This exact four-day schedule is a practical design, not a routine tested as one package. It gives back 12 direct working sets, side delts 8, rear delts 4 and upper chest 8 each normal week, while retaining lower-body work. Compound lifts involve other muscles too; these are not isolated doses.

ACSM’s 2026 update emphasizes consistent resistance training and matching volume to goals. A 2026 dose-response analysis supports volume benefits with diminishing returns. Neither makes a fixed number universally optimal. Adjust to your actual recovery. ACSM update · Volume analysis.

A trial comparing 1.6 and 3.2 g/kg protein did not show a general requirement for the higher intake. The higher-protein menus reflect your preference. Protein-dose trial.

Studies are often short with limited samples. Group averages cannot predict your individual tissue gain. The 16-week duration, review dates, warm-up sequence, exercise choices and small calorie steps are implementation choices. Current grocery prices and physical taste-testing have not been verified.
