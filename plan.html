The five-day routine

Repeat Upper, Lower, Rest, Chest + Back, Legs, Shoulders + Arms, Rest. The initial calendar starts with Upper on Monday; moving the rotation deliberately can change the weekdays. Rest remains part of the plan, and completion never advances the calendar automatically.

Upper and Lower begin with heavier work for strength. Chest + Back and Legs add moderate-repetition work. Shoulders + Arms has its own session. Keep the last session controlled: Saturday arms and shoulders also assist Monday's presses and pulls. Recovery intervals are opportunities, not guarantees.

Before your first main lift, use 2–4 progressively heavier warm-up sets with easy reps. Add a lighter rehearsal for later movements when useful. Warm-ups are not working sets.

Schoenfeld et al., 2016 compared one- and three-minute rests in 21 trained men; Singer et al., 2024 pooled nine studies and found uncertain, generally small differences in growth.

Weekly direct targets total chest 10, back 11, quads 10, hamstrings 8, calves 6, abs 4, side delts 5, rear delts 3, biceps 5 and triceps 5, plus two overhead-press sets. Squats, hinges and split squats also work the glutes. Pressing and pulling add indirect arm and shoulder work; these counts are planning estimates, not isolated biological doses.

If time runs short, prioritize the first main movements and save what you actually completed as partial. Do not rush heavy sets or mark unfinished work done. Review recovery before adding volume.

Open Train

Know when to add weight

Reps in reserve (RIR) means how many more clean repetitions you think you could complete. Three RIR leaves room; zero means no further clean rep. Use about 3 RIR while establishing a new setup, then work mostly at 1–2 RIR. The repeating program uses 2 RIR as its normal logging target. Failure is not required on every set: an eight-week lower-body study found similar quadriceps growth with 1–2 RIR and failure, with more acute fatigue at failure. Refalo et al., 2024.

  1. Pick a load that reaches the bottom of the rep range with the prescribed reserve and good form.
  2. Keep that load and add reps gradually. For 4 × 6–10, 10/10/9/8 means keep building.
  3. When every prescribed set reaches the top cleanly at the planned RIR, use the smallest practical increase next time. The repeating program uses a conservative 2 RIR threshold. Older templates retain their original calibration rules.
  4. If a new load drops you below the range, reduce it or use a smaller increment.

Keep equipment, seat height, range of motion, units and load convention consistent. For dumbbells, log one dumbbell; for barbells, include the bar. Follow each exercise’s Setup note. A different machine or variant starts a new comparison. Adding reps and adding load are both reasonable; neither must happen every workout. Progression trial.

Fatigue, substitutions and short weeks

Choose a comfortable alternative in the logger and keep it stable long enough to compare. If performance falls across several sessions while soreness, sleep or motivation worsen, first review food, sleep and outside workload.

A practical lighter week is about 5–7 days with half the usual sets and 3–4 reps left. The logger uses 4 RIR. Resume normal work when recovered. This is a fatigue-management option, not a required week off or a proven growth boost; a trial of complete cessation found no hypertrophy advantage. Deload trial.

Missed a day? The calendar keeps its planned rest days. In Train, deliberately move the rotation if you want to resume from a different day. Nothing creates a backlog or doubles your next workout. Optional easy walks, such as 20 minutes twice weekly, can fit around lifting if restorative. This is a scheduling idea, not an individualized cardio prescription. Ab work trains the trunk; it does not selectively remove waist fat.

The Sunday review

Weigh under similar morning conditions and compare weekly averages. A practical off-season gain range is 0.25–0.5% of body weight weekly—about 0.4–0.85 lb at 170 lb. Prefer the lower end for this leaner-gain goal; it is guidance, not a measured personal optimum. Off-season nutrition review.

The tracker needs six weigh-ins in each of two seven-day windows, at least 12 intake logs across them, and at least 90% calorie adherence within ±10% of target before offering a review signal. Below 0.25% or above 0.5% weekly gain prompts review. Confirm a persistent trend alongside waist, digestion, recovery, training and real intake before considering a small 100–150 kcal change.

Measure waist weekly at the same place and time; compare relaxed photos monthly if useful. Early scale gain can include water and food mass. Faster gain does not prove faster muscle growth: a small eight-week surplus trial found larger skinfold gains with faster weight gain, without consistent extra muscle or strength benefit. Helms et al., 2023.

Every few weeks, compare matched exercises, recovery and trends. Continue what works. Projections are informational; they never automatically raise calories to meet 195 lb or trigger a cut. No credible plan can promise 25 lb of lean tissue in 16 weeks.

Why this program, and the limits of the evidence

This five-day layout is a coaching choice. It distributes working sets across the week without assuming that more gym days produce more growth. Chest and back have two direct regional exposures; legs have two. Delts and arms also work indirectly during presses and pulls, so those exposures are not counted as additional full isolation sets.

Pelland et al., 2025/2026 pooled 67 studies with 2,058 participants, mostly young men. More volume was associated with gains but with diminishing returns. Schoenfeld et al., 2019 reviewed 25 studies: higher frequency added little hypertrophy when volume was matched. Ramos-Campo et al., 2024 pooled 14 studies and 392 adults and found similar outcomes for split and full-body routines at matched volume. These are group averages, not tests of this exact schedule.

Refalo et al., 2024 studied 18 trained adults for eight weeks: quadriceps growth was similar at 1–2 RIR and failure, with more fatigue at failure. Plotkin et al., 2022 randomized 43 trained adults and found that progressing repetitions or load both supported adaptations over eight weeks. Their short, specific protocols do not establish an exact personal prescription.

Testosterone research does not establish an enhanced-lifter split or a higher recoverable volume. Bhasin et al., 1996 studied 43 healthy men under short-term supraphysiologic exposure and demonstrated anabolic effects, but did not compare splits. Hildreth et al., 2013 studied 167 older men with low-normal testosterone for 12 months; that replacement context differs from healthy young men using supraphysiologic exposure. Neither establishes long-term safety or justifies automatically adding sets.

A trial comparing 1.6 and 3.2 g/kg protein did not show a general requirement for the higher intake. The higher-protein menus reflect your preference. Protein-dose trial.

Studies are often short with limited samples. Group averages cannot predict your individual tissue gain. The repeating calendar, review dates, warm-up sequence, exercise choices and small calorie steps are implementation choices. Current grocery prices and physical taste-testing have not been verified.
