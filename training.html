Before your first working set

Warm up for 5–8 minutes, then take 2–4 gradually heavier practice sets for your first compound lift. Warm-ups do not count toward the logged working sets. Rest 2–3 minutes on compounds and 60–120 seconds on smaller movements.

RIR = reps in reserve. 2 RIR means you could do about two more clean reps. Weeks 1–2 use 3 RIR; later aim for 1–2. Stop a set if technique breaks or a movement hurts.

Weekly targets & aiming for 60–75 minutes

Weekly working sets: chest 8, back 12, side delts 8, rear delts 4, quads 10, hamstrings 5 curls + 3 hinges, calves 6, abs 6; biceps and triceps each get 4 direct sets plus compound work. This is a starting point, not a quota to force when recovery is poor.

Upper days have 20 working sets; lower days have 16 and 14. Aim for 60–75 minutes, allowing for gym availability and the rest you need. Pair curls with triceps, or calves with abs, if it keeps technique and rest intact. If you reach your time limit, finish the session as partial and prioritize recovery. Do not rush the main lifts.

Keep the same exercise choices for several weeks when possible. Changing the machine, attachment, range or unit creates a separate comparison. Read the training rationale and sources.
